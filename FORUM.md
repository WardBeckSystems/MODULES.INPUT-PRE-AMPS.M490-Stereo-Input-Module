## Ward Beck pre's

_WBS INFO · May 26, 2007|40_

> Ward Beck had a design that worked and put the same thing in an entire series so the pre-amp is almost identical in all of the L series it's how the signal goes out of the module that is different. It really depends on here you tap the output. 
>
> The M470's are the most flexible in terms of routing, so if you're looking for a console with flexibility fit them with M470's. within the M470's their are several variations different aux buses, pan pots master outputs. The big one and most flexible is the M470C it has 4 Aux buses, 6 sub group sends and pan pot. since some consoles were used as mono only, some M470's dont even have a pan pot. All M470's are swappable, so if you have a console with them any variation will pop in without any problems. I keep a set of M470C's kicking around so when a M470A module in my console dies or needs service they are standing by. 
>
> The M490's are identical pre-amps as th M470's just smaller with a fader fitted in to the module. they were used in radio broadcast consoles. Limited routing and they have a very flexible remote button section. 
> The M480 is a great module it combines a bit of the L series pre-amp and the EQ of say a scaled back M472 (the M480 has fixed LF and HF points) the M480's were only fitted in the T1202 transportable mixer. 
>
> The M460's use a different transformer and a different booster amp setup various eq and routing options are availible on these modules, they make great rack mounted pieces
> The M461 is identical input section as M460's but has no EQ
> The M441's are the most sought after and the closest to the original neve design that was copied. Best transformers, detended pots very little circuit boards all hand made. great pieces! 
> The M600 is the same vintage as the M441's they were designed to be remote pre-amps or used in intercom racks. they share some components as the M441's but are a rack tray module with only a gain pot not a console module
>
> i hope this helps, and if I'm wrong on some details sorry in advance

## EQ comparison

_WBS INFO · Feb 28, 2008|41_

> well the difference is mainly it's functionality. Not every EQ has the same flexibility.
>
> M462 family sound the same as they are the same. The circuits are "ALMOST" identical. 
>
> M462 - 3 band full sweeps
> M462A - mid sweep and 2 fixed Q's on lf and hf
> M462B - 4 band full sweeps
>
> All the booster amps in the M462 series are the same, so they way it boosts the signal is identical.
> The M462 are balanced in nd balanced output so they could be patched in or put in line with the M470's insert send.
>
> Ward beck recycled circuits as most companies back then and still do. 
>
> The pre in the M490 is the SAME as the M470 and M480... the EQ in the M480 is derived from the M472
>
> The M472 is closest to the M462A, but with no mid sweep and a fixed high frequency. very simple. it is unbalanced and has no booster amp... the booster comes from the M470's EQ send and return. If you look closely at the M472 there are jumpers internally connected that short the IN and OUT where the M462's would plug in. Any "auxilary" that is balanced such as the M462, the M466 comps, M467 NG. M464 HP LP filters use the same in and out config. so on my console it's fitted all with M462's I could (if i wanted to) swap the EQ with a compressor... kinda cool!
>
> the M472 is simple and it shipped as the stock solution on most L472 consoles.
>
> I will say there are more EQ's like the M472A and the M472C that are stereo, then there are M522, M522A, M522B, M522C etc etc that are loosly based on the M462's.
>
> The EQ in the M460 is like the M462, infact they are the exact same cards. 
>
> In my opinion the M462A are the best, and having a bunch of M462B's have been really nice. 
>
> I will be selling a small rack of (6) M472's in the next week or so; my trip cost me more that I expected.

## Link to Gallery of custom M490 Rack Job in progress.

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Feb 4, 2009|42_

> http://gallery.me.com/tekbench/100056
> Some of the FAQ's:
> The patchbay is 1/4" Long Frame, not to be confused with regular 1/4". Sometimes called Military 1/4", and looks like a bigger version of the familiar TT (Tiny Telephone) connector.
> The modules are held in with Allen screws through the module into threaded holes in the aluminum rail. Hex screws for you outside the US.
> The aluminum rails are set into the sides of the box. This was tricky to get set to the correct depth, and we did it with a router and a few beers. No one was injured.
> Box is plain ol' finish plywood. Originally, we supposed we'd make a fancy cabinet, but decided to size it so it would fit INSIDE a standard 19" rack. the pictures do not show the rack ears installed yet. The rest of the studio has exposed edge plywood as an industrial detail, so that was indeed on purpose. Not much attention was paid to the finish. It's an environmentally friendly petroleum based oil product. We used it on all the wood in the studio. Pretty cool stuff. Low VOC.
> The modules do NOT have the NOS plates installed yet, but they're on the bench. This box is fully functional right now, but it's a little hack to use, but this is still 'in progress'. We haven't cleaned it up for show yet. We figured, unlike a lot of studios out in the world, that we'd make it work good first, and make it look good second.
> Yes, every solder point has a chunk of shrink tube over it. All solder points have a service loop of the same length.
> The cable used was all Belden; 9451 for all the patch bay connections and Brilliance 1800B for the mic connections to the back panel. 
> Not shown (but hinted at the red and black twisted harness) is the power supply. 625B yet to be mounted. INSIDE the box, connected to the standard IEC socket and fuse you see on the back panel is a FiveFish Studios +- 25, +48 supply. The FiveFish did not quite have enough juice to power the modules and the EQ's without dropping down to about 23.5v DC. So we're contemplating leaving it in there just for phantom +48.
> The back panel hides 3 M497 sum amplifiers that do summing for the Program 2 buss, aux buss and Program 1 bus (but that's overkill for us, so it may stay 'unused'. Direct PGM 1 outs AND 2 mix busses? too much for us.). The holes are to access the trim pots on the M497's.
> The rails holding the EDAC connectors on the inside of the box are standard Middle Atlantic lashing bars - LBP-1A. We took a hacksaw and cut them back a little, bent the rack ears 90 degrees and slid them inside the box. Bolted them in through the sides. The slots on the lashing bars allow side to side movement of the mounting screws. Button cap stainless screws with matching nuts on the connector side. Tricky to mount at first, but after its together, you have lots of adjustment.
> You can pull the modules just like on a regular console.
> The power is just buss wire soldered onto the pins. the red and black wires that run up to the top power the 2 upper EQ's.
> it's been a fun project so far. All the modules are dead silent with the exception of one that has a mild 60 cycle hum. Caps will probably fix that just fine.
> We're short an EQ. right now.
> The 'blank space' above the patch bay and below the modules is reserved for an aluminum strip that will have the phantom switches (1 per channel) and indicator LED's for phantom on or off. It's being milled up to have the switch holes, and a version of the Ward Beck logo on the far right under the EQ's. Should be pretty slick.
> So, there you have it. We're still tinkering a bit, but the damn thing works so good, It will probably get shoved into service ASAP.
> Tekbench.

> I think I took some pictures during that process... I'll dig them up.
> Essentially it is aluminum angle, 3/4"x3/4". I routed a 3/4" slot into each side panel before assembly, 1/4" deep (half the thickness of the ply) and toward the inside of the box the depth of the angle, plus the small reveal on the front edge. I routed a ?? channel, or dado (for you wood workers) in the top piece to match that depth toward the inside of the box. I sunk the upper rail into the lid a little further than ? the depth of the ply. I wanted the modules fairly tight to each side, and to the top of the box. I revealed more aluminum on the lower rail so I?d have something to attach the phantom switch plate to. I cut the angle to fit lengthwise, and tapped it in with a rubber mallet, stick, file, and patience. I then put some Gorilla Glue in the channel ends on the lower rail. On the top, I drilled through the top of the wood, and through the channel. Tapped the channel the same thread as rack screws, and countersunk some rack screws through the lid, into the rail 90 degrees to the angle aluminum, parallel to the module face, taking care to space them NOT directly behind a module screw.
> I was not a Gorilla Glue believer, but that stuff will stick chrome to a trailer hitch, or aluminum to wood. It was not necessary in the end, but if you pull all the modules, the bottom rail could possibly shift position, so we thought it safe to just put some in there for fun.
> I then lined the modules up in the box, spaced them across left to right. Since the outside of the box had to be 17.5? Maximum, we worked that dimension tight, and adjusted the spacing between the modules on the inside of the box. Marked the top and bottom holes with the modules laid out how we liked, and drilled and tapped them each by hand. It worked OK, but if I did it again, I?d order the stuff pre-machined with the tap holes exactly where I wanted them. Since the box is wood, its tough to pre-fit metal into it. Wood is not an exact science, and metal is pretty unforgiving.

## M460LA Manual

They were made for 1 customer and there were 2 large boards supplied. The boards made it to Dan Alexander this year and he parted them out. They were engineered not by Dad but the engineering department of building 1. They are a nightmare, very different internal wiring from the rest of the M460 series and Dad was not amused with that evolution, so much so that he waded back in and designed and directed every project form that point on except for the Renaissance consoles - he had absolutely nothing to do with those with the exception of suggesting the control room volume pot be an infinite rotation optical type pot. The M460M is a 3 band fully parametric EQ with an additional daughter amplifyer board jury-rigged in place to provide a line level feed to a FOH board. The boards were initially used for game shows, then 1 went off to do sound track work and the FOH mod was used to go to a smaller board for headphone mixes and producers feeds. 
> The stupidity of the M460M (OK I am rambling and wayyyy off topic) is the wiring of the Pendar switch (CH ON thing) it was not done to standard convention, which was done to save locating (1) Shadow switch to the extrusion, and floating it off the mother PCB (like the CUE on an M490 module)!!!

## Ward Beck m490c...and I'm brand new. Hi!

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Jul 31, 2012|45_

> Hello PB-Kid.
>
> Well the radio modules (M490 series) have the same basic pre-amplifyer as the M470, M480, and M500 series. A couple of slight tweaks here and there but essentially the same (same transformer as well). As Phil supplied you with the link to the schematic - start here. Learn the routing, the pin configurations, where to place the EQ, etc... A good modification would be is to replace the trim pot that sets the in-put gain of the microphone with a long-shaft pot. This way you can attenuate both the in-put and the put-put of the module - a little more conventional, and gives you a few options for colour. That would be R25 - the sensitivity. We set them at the factory, and often the radio station would calibrate them to their needs as transmission lines/methods would change of the years. A 10k pot should be sufficient, but watch the taper as if you use the wrong one all the attenuation will be at one end!!!
>
> AS per Phil's schematic link - drop the EQ at the insert point (P2 6 and P2 F for the send and the return P2 7 and P2 H).

## Sonic difference between the M441's and the M480's

_WBS INFO · Nov 9, 2012|45_

> Good afternoon Andrew in Montreal!
>
> Phil will assist you with the EQ's for the M460 series and the M402, or I can if you visited Toronto. Long story they are all very different. The first 124 modules had a large input transformer, custom designed by dad of course. That was a card that evolved in to a modular format very quickly. Same innards, different mounting. No sonic difference. They are the most broad in frequency response and resolution. Next are the M600's - of which I use regularly - they are a completely different beast and will not be discussed, but are the next in the chain of evolution. The M460 units of which I use regularly, have a significantly smaller transformer (custom, Dad, etc..), and I feel are the absolute unit for any mixing (they add a little weight to the tone / LF resolution). Tracking they are great as well but I prefer the M600's for that. Next are the M470, M480, M490, M500 series. They all have a smaller transformer 9custom, Dad, etc..). This was designed to meed the changing marketplace - price point. The pre-amplifyer is a little different as well. They all have a sound. They are all exceptional units. They will indeed out-perform British and American Ward Beck (I like ya kid!), but are a flavour. How the low frequencies are cradled is a great place to start the debate. No frequencies are hyped or exaggerated, nor will you measure any ringing on the units - thus no compensating capacitors are really required. I have done several records to tape on my old T1202A (M480) and they are super celan, give you what you give them (like B&K / Schopes microphones), the M460 series gives a little flavour as the low frequencies are snug, but allowed to bloom (think M49), then the M124 cards (real early stuff) well you know how when you wake up in the morning, you cant really see for like 2 - 3 seconds? But at mid day you can read your neighbours e-mails from across the room ..... well these units are the mid-day eyes. Pure transistor softness, no harshness, all frequencies are in-focus, available, and useable. 
>
> The EQ's are very, VERY different. The M402 series are step attenuators, where the later series are all op-amp based. They are musical, easily fit in the mix, solve an issue, bring a guitar out just a little bit, have the 80's snare BARK!!! The M402's if you get the recording or the film ones you are fine, if you get a broadcast one (more common) I advise changing the HF point from 6k to 12k, or put a switch outside the module to change the HF at the flip of a switch. I use mine for gtr and vox most times when EQ is needed. The EQ's found in the M460 modules (and the rest of them for that matter) are all the same concept as each other. They are consistently variable, some are full parametric, some are semi, etc... They are very clean do not add weight to the tone (the module does that nicely!). Using them independently of a trasnformered module you will not experience any tone degradation or addition. they do not really impart a sonic signature. So it will not conflict with your signal, just knob twiddling will!!
>
> As mentioned the M600/M500 units are very diffident. They are amplifyers that are transformer coupled on the input and the output with a mid-stage trim. Hit them hard. You will want many, many more!!
>
> Hope this helps. But the best is to bug someone with a couple of modules, get a CD with which you are intimately familiar, and listen to the programme through the modules.
